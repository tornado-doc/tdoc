Jev, explained simply

Three questions only: how people think it is built, whether it is actually good, and what people use it for. Architecture reverse-engineering and Mac mini numbers from research-claw (2026-09-20/21); launch use-cases from the first four days of public posts.

An ordinary model writes you a paragraph. Jev does not write. You hand it a form — questions with allowed answers — and it fills the form in, each answer with a confidence number. That is the product.

If it says 0.85, that is the confidence score: answers in that bucket should be right ~85% of the time — not a guarantee on this one row.

1. How do people think it is built?

TypeSafe says: pretrained language model → RLCD (Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions), their third branch after RLHF / RLVR. No weights public. Output tokens are free; jev-1.13.0 is $0.042 per million input tokens (docs.typesafe.ai/models). Outside guesses come from API probes (Archer Hume) and from the open clones that appeared within 48 hours and copied the interface.

Same socket shape on the right. Different guts on the left — especially context length and whether probabilities were ever trained to be honest.

Read: short-text clone benches are real. The jobs people are excited about (browser / phone) need long state + many options + calibrated "stop." That is where open copies fall over — not because the Mac is small (hardware explains kev-4b thrash; it does not explain wrong tickets or never saying done).

Still unchecked in public: Jev's calibration on your data. Bucket a few thousand labelled decisions and see if the 0.8 bucket is ~80% right. TypeSafe left public benchmarks blank on purpose at launch.

The use case that took off: context pruning — score old agent steps for whether they still matter so the expensive model only re-reads what counts (2.1M views in two days). Objection (@theo): pruning throws away context whose value you discover later.

Practical split from the measurements: short fixed labels + your own data → local kev/Nimble can be enough (add post-hoc calibration). Browser, phone, long docs, 20+ options → Jev; nothing open got close on those tasks.
