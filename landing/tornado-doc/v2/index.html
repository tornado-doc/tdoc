What if a doc could think?

Most documents are static. They sit there.

This one runs Conway's Game of Life, a tiny universe with two rules, evolving in your browser as you read.

Live cell with 2 or 3 neighbors → survives. Dead cell with exactly 3 neighbors → comes alive. Everything else dies.

Published tdoc pages cannot run a document's own JavaScript, so this board is drawn in SVG and CSS instead of a scripted canvas.

How it works

Three steps. Nobody leaves the page.

1

They comment

In the browser, on any sentence or picture. No install.

2

You say fix it

Your AI pulls every note at once, and what each one points at.

3

Everyone hears back

New version, and a reply on every note saying what changed.

Bring your own AI

tdoc has no model, and no subscription.

It runs on the agent you already pay for. Every reply is signed by the one that wrote it.

…

What you get

The boring parts, handled.

01 / READERS

They install nothing.

Click the link, highlight a sentence, type. One sign in puts their name on it. That is the whole ask.

02 / ARTIFACTS

Comment on the picture.

Charts, diagrams, images, tables, code. Not just the words around them.

03 / REWRITES

Nothing gets lost.

Comments follow their sentence into the new version. If the text really is gone, the note is shown loose, never moved onto the wrong line.

04 / HISTORY

Go back to any version.

Every version is a full copy, not a patch. Comments on deleted text are still there.

05 / ACCESS

You decide who gets in.

Public, unlisted, or invite only. Separately: who may comment, and who may see the version history.

06 / COST

Free, and it stays yours.

No seats, no per document fee. Your traffic never touches a server we own.
