Competitor analysis

Forty-two products screened, five worth plotting. Everything that already has your context is built for one person; everything built for a team makes you paste it in.

Build for the empty quadrant — team-shaped, running inside the session that already holds the context. Nobody is there, and it is the only position the other four cannot reach.

TL;DROf 42 products, none sit in team-shaped and already-has-your-context. Five come close on one axis and fail the other.

Why the empty quadrant is empty

The solo tools sit inside your editor, which is exactly why they have your context and exactly why nobody else can comment.

The team tools own the comment thread and nothing else. They never see the repo, the data or the session the work actually happens in.

tdocagent-context/v5 ▾

Design: how the agent gets your context

The 200k window is full before tdoc asks for anything: repo 68k, open threads 36k, connectors 44k, the doc itself 28k, 24k free.

Trim the repo first. It is the only source large enough to matter and the easiest to rank down, and it returns 44k of headroom without dropping a connector.

TL;DRThe repo is a third of the window. The doc being edited is 14%, and free space is 12%.

What we are cutting first

Nothing here is a new integration. Every source is already open in the session before tdoc is asked for anything.

The budget is the design constraint: once the window is full, the oldest thread silently falls out and nobody is told.

Features

Why the round actually closes.

01 / FIXES

Apply every comment, yours and your team’s, in one click.

Twelve comments, one instruction. It reads them all at once, edits the page, and closes each one out, so a review round stops eating an afternoon.

02 / CONTEXT

It already knows your context.

It runs inside the agent session you already have open, so every connector your team has already wired up — Slack, GitHub, Drive, Sheets, your analytics — is context it can read. “Fix the numbers” reaches the real numbers.

03 / VERSIONS

Every change is on the record.

Every version shows what changed, when, and which comment asked for it. When your manager asks whether you addressed the feedback, the doc is the answer.

04 / BRANDING

Your brand, applied for you.

Point tdoc at your brand once — fonts, colours, logo, the way your charts look — and it builds a template out of them: letterhead, headings, footer, page furniture. Every doc after that comes out on that template, official enough to send outside the company without anyone reformatting it.
